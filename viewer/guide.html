01 / THE EXPERIMENTS

Four small positions.
Four changes of mind.

01

Pinned is not powerless

May White’s king step onto f5 when the knight attacking it is pinned?

The rook on e1 pins the knight on e7 to the black king. The knight still attacks f5 for king-safety purposes. Try Kf5, then move the rook to a1 and repeat. Removing the pin changes the knight’s freedom; it does not remove its attack.

Key distinction: attack relation ≠ legal movement. FIDE 3.1.3 and 3.9.2.

02

The capture that opens a rank

Why is gxf6 en passant illegal when the destination looks safe?

The white pawn leaves g5 while the black pawn disappears from f5. Both blockers leave the rook’s fifth rank, exposing the white king on h5. Move the rook from a5 to a4: the same capture becomes legal.

Key distinction: move semantics include removals away from the destination. FIDE 3.7.3 and 3.9.2.

03

When a queen brings a draw

Does choosing the most valuable promotion always help?

On c8, a queen produces stalemate. A rook leaves Black the reply Ka6. A bishop or knight produces an insufficient-material draw in this fixture. This demonstrates terminal outcomes and available continuation; it makes no engine claim that rook promotion forces a win.

Key distinction: material value is subordinate to terminal rules. FIDE 3.7.3.3, 5.2.1 and 5.2.2.

04

The square the king must cross

Can White castle when e1 and g1 are safe, but f1 is attacked?

Castling includes a transit condition. A black rook on f8 attacks f1, so the king cannot cross it. Move that rook to a8 and test the same castling move again.

Key distinction: a permitted endpoint does not establish a permitted transition. FIDE 3.8.2.2.

02 / THE METHOD

Prediction → intervention → witness.

Predict

State the move and expected outcome before pressing a button. Which relation is carrying your argument?

Intervene

Use the counterfactual selector. It changes the relevant rook placement while preserving the question. Compare the explanation and the actual legal move set.

Inspect

Reset and compare. Export FEN, the observation JSON, or a static X3D position. The seven source positions have saved Python witnesses independently checked in chess.js.

The useful scientific habit is to save the claim, its input, a positive witness, and a nearby case where the result changes. Matching two implementations helps detect bugs; independent known-answer cases help detect a shared misconception.

The four lessons contain seven source positions and sixteen explicit move probes. The catalog is byte-hashed. Damaged legality and terminal-state twins are rejected by the verification controls. The viewer does not silently replace an unavailable engine with replay.

03 / READING THE ARENA

A view is an argument.
Read its assumptions.

InstrumentWhat it representsHow to read it
Stockfish scoreEngine search of a particular pre-move FEN, displayed from White’s perspective.Inspect its source ply. A score attached to the position before a move must not be read as an evaluation of the displayed position after that move. Mate scores are separate from centipawns; null remains unavailable.
Attack balanceWhite attack count minus Black attack count at each square.This is a geometric pressure proxy. Pinned pieces still contribute attacks. It is not a probability of capture, legal mobility, or a solved tactical evaluation.
Tactic linksStatic pin/fork/skewer candidates and legal discovered-check witnesses.A motif is a candidate. Search and adversarial replies are required before declaring a combination sound or a move best.
Feature spaceA three-coordinate heuristic embedding of material, attack-count space, and king shelter.Coordinates clip at ±5 and scale by 0.7. Different positions can map to the same point. Distances are not calibrated chess similarity or physical trajectories.
Large score swingA thresholded difference between successive compatible score samples.A swing is not a blunder verdict. Search depth, score bounds, mate transitions, and position identity matter.
ReplayScripted legal games with a material proxy.The mode is visibly labeled. It does not supply Stockfish judgments.

Stockfish’s centipawn normalization is engine-specific. A score is not a literal promise that the advantage equals a recoverable pawn or a calibrated win probability for the person watching.

04 / THE PORTABLE OBJECT

A position you can take apart.

The Position .x3d button writes ordinary X3D 4.0 geometry with explicit piece occupancy, coordinate labels, the selected palette, and optional study relations. Each piece has a square-bearing name such as piece_h5. Snapshot metadata records the FEN and scope.

The owned export removes X_ITE’s Shape.pointerEvents extension and reports that projection. It does not serialize Workers, search trees, animation state or repetition history. Import fidelity must be measured separately from XML validity.

FEN is a position record, not the whole experiment. It includes side to move, castling rights, an en passant field and move clocks, but no full repetition history. The en passant lesson retains the raw input because libraries normalize away an en passant target when no legal capture exists.

For Blender and other importers, begin with a portable example in examples/x3d/. Check piece count and square centers after axis conversion, then inspect materials, text, relation geometry and camera framing. A studio render can demonstrate an import path; it does not certify native browser rendering.

05 / RUN, VERIFY, EXTEND

Keep the evidence close.

python3 run_chess.py --study --open
# or serve viewer/ with any local HTTP server

# verification in an isolated Python environment
python3 -m pip install -r server/requirements-dev.txt -r tools/requirements-validation.txt
npm test

The repository includes the review, renderer and protocol contracts, an integration atlas, a small ontology vocabulary, executable controls, and machine-readable evidence. Checks distinguish chess rules, event transport, generated geometry, actual engine execution, importer fidelity, and native pixels.

Optional Analyst workspace: choose Detail → Analyst for an unobstructed board and a separate instrument desk. Position shows material, mobility and score provenance; Structure lists passed, isolated and doubled pawns, file occupancy and king-ring pressure; Control provides 64 exact attack balances linked to the X3D inspector. Search holds one game while requesting 1, 3 or 5 legally replayed engine lines at a chosen depth. Release resumes that game. The other side’s mobility is a hypothetical turn projection without castling or en passant, and is unavailable when the real side is in check.

Use the Camera selector for three-quarter, overhead and Black-side focus. The Palette selector gives direct access to each theme. Arrow keys within the numeric control matrix navigate its squares; elsewhere the existing inspector and board shortcuts apply. Search arcs connect successive destinations in a variation, rather than the trajectory of one piece. Ordinary score graphs have gaps for missing scores, mate reports and nonexact bounds.

Rendering evidence: native browser checks and software-rendered screenshots are recorded separately from rules and XML validation. A successful local headless rendering check does not establish target-device GPU performance. Run node tools/desk-smoke.mjs and inspect its receipt for the new workspace.

Useful next experiments include measured MultiPV counterfactuals at equal search budgets, proof-bearing tactical exercises, accessible keyboard square inspection, and scaling trials at 1, 10, 20 and 64 boards. Each deserves a separate acceptance condition.
